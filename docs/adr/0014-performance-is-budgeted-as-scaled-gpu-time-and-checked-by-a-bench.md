# Performance is budgeted as scaled GPU time on a discrete GPU and checked by hand with a bench

v1 must run smoothly on integrated graphics, but the driving dev has no integrated GPU to measure, and CI renders on Mesa's software rasteriser (ADR-0005), whose timings mean nothing. So the integrated-GPU target is turned into a budget on hardware that *can* be measured. The reference is an Intel Iris Xe (96 EU) class iGPU, assumed to be **1/20 of an RTX 4070**. That's conservative: it is about 14× behind on compute and 7× on bandwidth. At 60 fps, the GL renderer gets 10 ms of the 16.7 ms frame, leaving the rest for readback, Prism compositing and the UI. That gives a budget of **0.5 ms of GPU time on the 4070 for the most expensive scene, at a 1920 × 1080 tray viewport, at the default quality preset.** A dev-only `--bench` mode measures it with GL 3.3 core timer queries, and running the bench is a release-checklist item that blocks a release, not a CI gate. The measured scenes (0.42 ms for the most expensive tray scene and 0.32 ms for a frame-filling translucent swirl, both at 1280 × 800 on the 4070; ADR-0009, ADR-0010) put full quality at about twice this budget at 1080p. The default preset is therefore cheaper than full quality, and the [What are v1's performance targets and caps?](https://github.com/evildarkarchon/Andys-Dice/issues/22) ticket fixed the caps and inherited numbers around this budget.

## Decisions

- **Reference and floor.** Iris Xe-class integrated GPUs get 60 fps at the default preset. Weaker DX11-class integrated GPUs (UHD 620) are best effort: usable at the Low preset, with no frame-rate promise.
- **Quality presets.** The player chooses **Low, Medium or High**, with no Custom. **Medium is the default**, and it is defined as whatever meets the 0.5 ms budget in `--bench`. High is the "looks best on a discrete GPU" preset. The values of each preset's levers are tuned against the bench and are not contracts.
- **Levers.** A preset sets ADR-0002's render scale, MSAA samples and shadow quality. It also sets the parallax occlusion sample counts (ADR-0008: 12–48 profile samples plus 12 for the shadow march at full quality) and the translucent march's sample count (ADR-0009: 8 at full quality).
- **Pixel cap.** Render scale applies to physical pixels. Before render scale applies, the render target is capped at about **2.07 million pixels** (1920 × 1080), keeping the viewport's aspect ratio, and JavaFX scales the frame up when it is shown. High lifts the cap. Without it, a maximised viewport on a 4K screen at 150% scaling (about 4.6 million pixels) would blow the budget twice over.
- **Frame pacing.** Playback, orbit and zoom render on JavaFX's default **60 Hz** pulse (ADR-0002 renders on demand). There is no high-refresh mode in v1. Each frame interpolates the recording, so ×4 playback and other refresh rates stay smooth.
- **Slow-GPU notice.** During playback the renderer measures each frame's GPU time with timer queries. If it stays over **14 ms** across a Throw, the app posts a **one-time notice behind the bell** (the Roll UI, `docs/v1-spec.md`) suggesting a lower preset. It never changes the preset itself, because the preset is the player's persisted choice (ADR-0007).
- **Dice per Throw.** The cap is **20 physical Dice**, a constant in code that the grammar, the tests and the soak test read. It is not a player setting. This fixes the provisional cap in the grammar for [What is the v1 Roll Expression grammar?](https://github.com/evildarkarchon/Andys-Dice/issues/7). ADR-0013 measured 1.3–1.7 attempts per Throw at 20 Dice, and 2.1 at 25 is the headroom kept in reserve.
- **Roll Expression length.** At most **100 characters**. Longer input fails like any other grammar error, with the span running from character 101 to the end. A realistic worst case is about 50.
- **Physics.** Jolt steps at **240 Hz**, and an attempt that runs past **4 s of simulated time** has timed out. Throws come to rest in 0.7–0.85 s. A timed-out attempt costs about 18 ms, so 24 of them cost about 0.45 s. The prototype's 12 s cap would have cost about 1.3 s.
- **Recording.** The recording keeps every 2nd step (**120 Hz**). The renderer interpolates positions linearly and orientations by slerp. At 60 Hz, a fast-tumbling Die can turn about 90° between samples. 20 Dice for 2 s at 240 Hz would still be only about 270 KB.
- **Throw duration.** There is no wall-clock target. The soak test fails if the **95th percentile of simulated time from release to rest exceeds 1.5 s** (a tunable), which catches physics tuning that makes Throws sluggish. The pickup before a Reroll Throw or Cocked Rethrow stays ADR-0010's fixed animation (about 1.3 s).
- **Enter to first motion.** For the realistic worst case (20 Dice, with an `rr` condition matching half the faces), the time from Enter to the first motion is at most **150 ms** on the driving dev's machine. `--bench` measures it, and a miss blocks the release just as the GPU budget does. v1 doesn't pipeline later Throws (which ADR-0006 allows), and adds pipelining only if the bench misses this target.
- **Sound.** The mixer renders **480-frame blocks** (10 ms at 48 kHz) and keeps **40 ms** of audio queued in the sink by default, both tunable. If the release checklist hears glitches, the queue first goes up to 60 ms before the OpenAL fallback is considered. That still sits inside ADR-0012's window (no more than 45 ms late) once the A/V offset is applied.
- **Soak size.** The soak test throws **1,000 Throws of 20 Dice into each built-in Tray** (3,000 in all, under a minute on a runner), nightly and on a `v*` tag. The count is a tunable.
- **Startup and memory.** It takes at most **3 s** from launch to an interactive window on the driving dev's machine. `--smoke-test` logs the time without gating on it, and the release checklist checks it. v1 sets no memory target and keeps the JVM's default heap.

## Considered Options

- **Measuring on a real integrated GPU.** Not possible, because no machine with one is available. This was ruled out in the renderer bake-off ([Renderer bake-off: JavaFX 3D vs embedded renderer d20](https://github.com/evildarkarchon/Andys-Dice/issues/8)).
- **A GPU-time gate in CI.** Rejected. CI renders with Mesa on the CPU (ADR-0005), so its timings say nothing about any GPU.
- **Stepping the preset down automatically at run time.** Rejected. It would quietly overwrite a persisted choice, and a single slow Throw (a driver hiccup, or a background load) would cost the player quality. The notice leaves the choice with the player.
- **Picking a preset on first run from the GL renderer string, or from a warm-up benchmark.** Rejected. A renderer-string table goes stale, and a warm-up adds startup time to every first launch. Medium is safe on the reference hardware anyway.
- **A Custom preset that exposes every lever.** Rejected for v1. Five levers interact in ways a player can't judge, and three presets can each be checked on the bench.
- **A high-refresh mode (120/144 Hz).** Rejected for v1. It doubles the GPU cost of every frame on hardware that is already the constraint, and the interpolated recording already looks smooth at 60 Hz.
- **25 Dice per Throw.** Rejected. It would cost about 30% more attempts and 25% more pixels of Dice, for a Throw no common D&D roll needs.

## Consequences

- **Amends ADR-0002.** The quality levers are grouped into three presets, and the parallax occlusion and translucent march sample counts become levers too. The render target is capped at about 2.07 million pixels below High, and playback paces to JavaFX's 60 Hz pulse.
- **Amends ADR-0003.** The step rate is 240 Hz, the simulated-time cap is 4 s, and the recording samples at 120 Hz.
- **Amends ADR-0005.** `--bench` joins the gallery as a dev-only mode, and running it is a release-checklist item that blocks a release. The soak test runs 1,000 Throws per built-in Tray and also gates on the 95th-percentile rest time.
- **Amends ADR-0012.** Blocks are 480 frames, and the sink queues 40 ms by default.
- **Persisted state (ADR-0007).** `settings.properties` keeps the quality setting as one preset key, and an unknown value falls back to Medium.
- **Every cost is a budget line.** Any new per-pixel effect must fit inside Medium's 0.5 ms, or come with a lever that each preset sets.
- **The 1/20 ratio is an assumption.** If anyone ever measures a real Iris Xe, the ratio, and with it the budget, should be replaced by the measurement.
